## RapidTest
## Evaluation of three rapid tests (RDTs) and ELISA IgM for human leptospirosis diagnosis.
## Aim
The goal of this study was to identify the most effective serological screening method to enhance case detection and improve national leptospirosis surveillance in Colombia.
## Abstract
As a country endemic for leptospirosis, Colombia requires a rapid diagnostic tool that surpasses the limitations of traditional methods like the Microscopic Agglutination Test (MAT) or culture. While Leptospira ELISA is currently used locally, its performance in specific populations remains uncertain. To assess the diagnostic accuracy of the Panbio Leptospira IgM ELISA in a Colombian setting, we evaluated 479 suspected leptospirosis cases. Using MAT as the gold standard, the ELISA demonstrated a sensitivity of 75% and a specificity of 71.92%, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.78. These results indicate limited diagnostic accuracy, particularly for detecting acute infections. Our findings highlight the need for further evaluation of rapid diagnostic tests (RDTs) for leptospirosis in Colombia. While RDTs can be useful tools, their interpretation should be cautious, considering factors like symptom onset and epidemiological context. Future studies should explore the potential of other RDTs with improved sensitivity and specificity to enhance the early diagnosis and management of leptospirosis.
## Methodology 
# Study design
A retrospective study evaluates the sensitivity and specificity of commercially available Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs) for detecting antibodies specific to Leptospira spp. Patient serum samples used in this study were submitted to the routine serology laboratory through the national leptospirosis surveillance at the reference laboratory Microbiology Group INS.
# Sample Size
To validate each individual Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) and Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) against the Microscopic Agglutination Test (MAT) serology results, the required sample size for testing one proportion (either sensitivity or specificity) was calculated. 

# Serum samples collection
The samples are transported under refrigeration to the Instituto Nacional de Salud (INS) laboratory from public health laboratories across the country. Upon arrival, they are maintained at 4°C until processing. Aliquots are stored at -80°C and only thawed once for further testing. Diagnostic Microscopic Agglutination Test (MAT) testing is conducted within 1 to 10 days after the samples are registered in the INS system, covering the period from January 2015 to December 2018. In the criteria parameters were considered, paired serum samples with a minimum volume of 1 ml available for both serum samples, with a time interval of at least 1 to 10 days between the onset of symptoms and the date of sample collection. Additionally, the second sample must be submitted 10 to 15 days after the first sample collection.

# Reference diagnostic
The Microscopic Agglutination Test (MAT) was conducted and interpreted according to the World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines. The Ellinghausen-McCullough-Johnson-Harris (EMJH) medium was initially prepared according to the manufacturer's instructions, followed by sterility testing and incubation at 37°C for 24 hours. After incubation, the EMJH supplement was added, and 9 ml of the medium was dispensed into screw-cap tubes. These tubes were then inoculated with live Leptospira serovars from a panel of 29 serovars. The identity test involved diluting the antisera and adding the prepared antigen. This mixture was incubated for two hours at 28°C and examined under a dark field microscope. Serum samples were diluted to a ratio of 1:50 with each cultivated strain. A volume of 50 μl of diluted serum (or SAF alone for control) and 50 μl of antigen diluted in a flexible plastic U-bottom microplate were added and incubated at 28°C for two hours. Samples were considered reactive when ≥50% agglutination of Leptospira was observed. Reactive samples were then serially diluted, starting from 1:100 to 1:3200, to determine the titter. A positive titter was defined as the dilution at which 50% agglutination was observed. A titer of at least 1:400 determined a confirmed case by MAT in a single sample or a fourfold increase between admission and convalescent samples.

# ELISA IgM methodology
The Panbio® ELISA IgM was employed to test the serum samples, following the manufacturer's instructions meticulously. Initially, a 1:100 dilution of the samples and controls was prepared using the diluent solution. Subsequently, 100 μl of each diluted sample was added to the wells and incubated for 30 minutes at 37°C. After incubation, the wells were washed four times with the wash solution. Then, 100 μl of the conjugate was added to each well, followed by another 30-minute incubation at 37°C and four subsequent washes. Next, 100 μl of TMB chromogen was added to each well and incubated at room temperature for 10 minutes. This was followed by the addition of 100 μl of the stop solution. The biochemical reading was conducted at 450 and 630 nm wavelengths in the read well. The result was calculated by dividing the absorbance of the sample by the absorbance of the standard and multiplying by the factor provided by the manufacturer. The results were interpreted as follows: values above 1.1 U/ml were considered positive; values between 0.9 and 1.1 U/ml were deemed inconclusive or undetermined (gray area); and values below 0.9 U/ml were interpreted as negative.

# Rapid diagnostic serological test (RDT)
Test-It Life Assay
Test-it™ IgM Leptospira human lateral flow test manufactured by LifeAssay is the RDT was used. This test can be performed with whole blood or serum samples; the result was read between 10 -15 minutes. The manufacturer information references a sensitivity of 100% and a specificity of 95.3%.  The procedure was to add 5 μl of the serum sample to the cassette plus 3 drops (80 μl) of running fluid (buffer). The reading of the results was obtained in 10-15 minutes. The interpretation of the results: positive result (one red line at the test zone (T) and a line at the control zone (C)); negative result (indicated by the absence of a line at the test zone (T) and presence of a line at the control zone (C)). If a test is run and no line appears at the control zone (C), the test should be re-run with a new cassette.

# Leptocheck WB
The rapid tests were performed according to the manufacturer's instructions. For the LeptoTek Lateral Flow 5 µl serum was spotted in the sample port of the device, running buffer was added and the test was read after 10 minutes. For the Leptocheck-WB 10 µl serum was spotted in the sample port of the device and 15 minutes after running buffer was added the test was read. Both tests were valid when the control band stained. Valid tests were scored positive when a test band was observed, negative when no band was observed and indeterminate when it was unclear whether a band was observed or not. Invalid tests were repeated. For the LeptoTek Dri Dot 10 µl of serum was mixed with the dried leptospiral-antigen-coated latex spot on the agglutination card. The test was read within 30 seconds and scored positive when agglutination was observed, negative when there was no agglutination and indeterminate when occurrence of agglutination was unclear.

# SD Bioline
The SD Bioline Leptospira IgG/IgM Test, an immunochromatographic assay, was utilized for the qualitative and differential detection of IgG and/or IgM antibodies to Leptospira interrogans. Using a 5 µl capillary pipette, 5 µl of the serum specimen was drawn up to the black line and added to the square sample well marked "S". Subsequently, 4 drops of assay diluent were added to the round-shaped assay diluent well. The test results were interpreted after 20 minutes.

# Data analysis
The overall accuracy of RDTs for diagnosing leptospirosis for serum samples was estimated following the STARD 2015 [14] (Supplementary). Positive MAT results under were considered true positives for leptospirosis for the purpose of gold standard analysis. The diagnostic accuracy of RDTs for diagnosing leptospirosis was defined by sensitivity and specificity with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The ELISA IgM assay was examined by using Receiver Operator Characteristic (ROC) plots, to determine the correctly identified to be positive and the fraction falsely identified to be positive according to manufacturer cut-off values. ROC area under the curve (AUC) were calculated as the fraction “correctly identified to be positive” and the fraction “falsely identified to be positive” determined according to manufacturer cut-off values for positive results. Samples with indeterminate results were excluded. Cohen’s kappa (K coefficient) was calculated to determine the degree of agreement between the two tests. Statistical analyses were performed using R Study.
